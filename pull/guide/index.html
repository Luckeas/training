Why pull-ups?

Pull-ups are the number one exercise in most dragon boat fit tests. In my coaching, the more pull-ups a paddler can do, the faster they are in the boat, up to a point, and it's rare to see an athlete who does too many. They also build your grip and your connection to your back and lats.

2 · The three levels

Train for your number

0

At zero: hold, then fight gravity

Jump up to the top and hold as long as you can. When you can't hold on any longer, don't let go: fight gravity all the way down, and keep fighting. Notice where you drop the fastest, and on your next set, try to hold right there. Build up to about 3 hard sets.

You're close whenYou can hold the top for about 30 seconds. Can't hold it for even a few seconds? That's when a band makes sense.
1–3

One to three: sets of one

Do your pull-ups in sets of just one rep, and mix in holds: about half your sets are a hold at the top for time. In week 1, that's 4 sets: 2 single reps and 2 holds. Add a set slowly.

4–12

The set that takes you from 4 to 12

Four to 12: sets of half your max, and a density set

Do sets of about half your max: at 4, sets of 2; at 10, sets of 5. Pull every rep as fast as you can. Start with 3 sets and build to about 5. In weeks 3 and 4, if your sets feel easy, add a rep to each set. After the retest, start again at half your new max. Every so often, do a density set: set a timer for 11 to 16 minutes and do as many pull-ups as you can. Keep notes and track it from week to week.

Around 12 to 15, it's time to add weight. That's part 2.

The long road

The longest road is from 0 to 1

Then your next goal is 1 to 4. And the road from 4 to 12? That takes weeks, not years.

  • Retest every 4 to 5 weeks, not every time you train, so you can actually see the progress.
  • Bands are for extra volume, or until you can hold the top on your own. After that, don't make them your main tool: you need hard sets at the level you're at right now.
  • Mix up your pulls. Different pull exercises build the muscles for the pull-up. A beginner doesn't need much to improve.
  • Your bodyweight is the weight. In a pull-up you lift your whole body, so leaner paddlers have it easier. Carrying extra weight? The plan still works, it just takes longer, and losing some body fat makes every rep easier.

3 · Your week

Your week, at your level

Pull-ups every time you're in the gym, three times a week, then pulldowns and rows. Heavy, light and medium days, so you don't do the same thing three times. Pick a day. Your 4 weeks are right below it.

This is the pull-up part of your week, not a full workout. It's the main work to build your pull-ups. Keep training the rest of your body around it: legs, pushing and core.

Week 5: retest

Test your max, put it in the calculator above, and start again at your new level. Then send me your new number.

  • 3 to 6 sets of pull-up work in a workout, holds included, even as your sets get bigger.
  • 2 to 4 sets of each other exercise, by how you feel.
  • Banded exercises can also be done without the band.

4 · The exercises

The 5 pulls in your plan

Where you see three rep ranges, they do different jobs: 3 to 5 heavy for the strength to do pull-ups, 8 to 12 lighter for muscle, 15+ light for endurance.

Warm up with banded scap pulls, leaning back. Press and lean back, chest facing the bar, and pull with your elbows slightly forward, not flared out. From a dead hang your shoulder can feel tight and stuck; leaning back frees it up so it moves smoothly.

  1. My favourite: the V-grip

    Lat pulldowns

    The closest thing to the pull-up
    • With a V-grip, hands together and wrists neutral, I feel my lats way more than on a regular pulldown.
    3–5heavy8–12lighter15+light

  2. Bench pulls

    Lats, traps, biceps, forearms
    3–5heavy8–12lighter15+light

  3. One-arm rows

    Lats, shoulder blades, forearms
    3–5heavy8–12lighter15+light

  4. Body rows and holds

    All the muscles above, plus connective tissue · max reps, then max time
    • Hold at the top. When you can't, hold in the middle, then just before your arms go straight. Or hold one position for time.
    • It gets harder as you lower the bar or raise your feet.

  5. Hammer curls

    Brachialis, forearms
    3–5heavy8–12lighter15+light

Using a band? Banded holds and negatives work the same way as the ones without. Do a few negatives first to find where you struggle most, then focus your holds on those spots.
