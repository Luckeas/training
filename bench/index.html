Why bench, if paddling is a pull?

Your pull is the engine of the stroke, and most of your power comes from it. Bench builds the general strength behind it and the push of your top arm. Train both, every week.

2 · The three things

What took her from 85 to 160

1

More volume

She benched 3 days a week, 5 to 8 sets each time. Each week she did a little more work at the same weight.

How

Bench 3 days a week. Days 1 and 2 start with 1 to 3 heavy top sets, then 3 to 4 lighter sets done fast. Day 3 is for building muscle.

2

Never skip the accessories

Triceps supersets to drive the bar up, and upper back work to keep her stable, on the bench and in the boat.

How

A triceps superset (cable pushdowns, then overhead cable extensions) and upper back work (chest-supported rows or seal rows), 3 sets of 10 to 12 each. In the plan below, they go on day 3.

Strong triceps finish the press. A strong upper back gives you a stable base to press from, and helps your posture in the boat.

3

The opposite of most paddlers

Every set lighter than her max, with a pause

All of her sets were submaximal, with a pause on the chest to build stability. Every session she got a little stronger and the reps moved faster. Only then did she add weight and start the whole process again.

How

Top sets stay between 80 and 90% of your max, from singles to triples: heavy, but never a grind. The rest are lighter sets at 60 to 70%, paused on your chest and pressed as fast as you can. After a lighter 4th week, add 5 to 10 lb (2.5 to 5 kg) and start again.

Why it works: the pause takes away the bounce, so you learn to own the bottom, where most reps get stuck. And because you never miss, you recover fast enough to bench 3 days a week.

The secret

Consistency beats maxing out

She trained hard, and she kept showing up. Every week built on the one before. Consistency compounds.

What makes showing up easier

  • Sleep 7 to 9 hours. That's where most of your recovery happens.
  • Eat enough overall. A big calorie deficit and a rising bench rarely go together.

3 · Your 4 weeks

Your first 4 weeks, in your numbers

Three different bench days each week: heavy, medium and light. Pick your week to see exactly what to do on each day. Every weight comes from the calculator.

Best in the off-seasonDo this plan in the off-season, when you have time to build strength. It's not recommended during the race season.

Example weights. Put your own set in the calculator above.

No grinding and no missed reps, even on top sets. The fast sets should feel light: that's on purpose. Press every rep as fast as you can.

  • Spread your 3 days through the week, for example Monday, Wednesday and Friday.
  • Warm up with a few lighter sets, working up to your top sets.
  • Rest about 3 minutes after top sets and 2 minutes between fast sets.
  • On fast sets, pause on your chest, then press as fast as you can.
  • Already on a team gym program? Do these bench days in place of the bench work there, not on top of it.
  • Use a spotter or set the safety pins, especially on top sets.
